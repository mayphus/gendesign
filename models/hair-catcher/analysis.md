## Checks

- At the 2.2 mm plate and 51% open: fingertip deflection 0.088 mm (limit 0.5), hard-push rib stress 15.5 MPa (allowable 20). Structure is close: 1.29x on the tighter of the two, so the plate stays at 2.2 mm (minimum 1.94).
- Drainage binds: 408 mm² of open area is 51% of the bore.
- A flat plate with 1.5 mm ribs reaches that with holes of about 5.3 mm inscribed (hexagonal estimate, ±15%). A 47.0° cone has 1.47x the area, so holes can drop to the 4.0 mm floor.
- Hole pattern: relaxed Voronoi needs 5.3 mm; regular hex grid needs 5.3 mm (6 cells cut under the 4.0 mm floor at the wall); round holes, hex grid needs 6.7 mm (27% larger); round holes, square grid needs 10.8 mm (105% larger).

## Concepts

- **flat plate** (chosen): Prints top-down with nothing over air and sits flush. Meets the flow target with ~5.3 mm holes; drainage is the active constraint, so the search should spend its freedom on open area, not thickness.
- **cone** (fallback): 1.47x the grate area: holes down to the 4.0 mm floor for the same flow. Costs ~18 mm of insert depth and runs the underside at the 45° overhang limit. Use it if the owner wants holes under the flat-plate size.
- **two-level** (rejected): The fine layer catches more hair but lowers flow, and it bridges (pebble-c: ~189 mm² over air) or needs vaults (pebble-d). Not needed while the flow target is met by one layer.

Chosen: **flat plate**, generated by `pebble-plate`.

## Pattern

Hole size each pattern needs for the same open area (smaller is better capture).

| Pattern | Hole needed | Verdict | Why |
|---|---:|---|---|
| relaxed Voronoi | 5.3 mm | chosen | Near-hexagonal cells: the least rib per open area (honeycomb), equal rib width everywhere, and cells mirrored across the wall end at the round boundary with no slivers. Can be graded. |
| regular hex grid | 5.3 mm | rejected | Same hole size as Voronoi, but the round wall cuts the grid: 13 full cells and 6 slivers that either hold water or must be filled, losing open area. |
| round holes, hex grid | 6.7 mm | rejected | Round holes leave solid corners between them, so they must be larger for the same flow: worse capture. |
| round holes, square grid | 10.8 mm | rejected | Even more solid between holes than the hex grid. |
| slots | — | rejected | A narrow width passes the ball test, but hair runs along a slot; the capture proxy does not apply. Long ribs are also the weakest in bending. |
| radial spokes | — | rejected | Openings widen toward the rim, so the outermost hole sets capture. |

Chosen pattern: **relaxed Voronoi**.
